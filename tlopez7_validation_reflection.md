# Validation Reflection 

I validated the ten MiniProject2 projects owned by jmurph91, and jmurph91 validated my ten. Across all 100 records the two of us agreed on 78 before discussion and on all 100 after. Agreement varied a lot by field. Currentstatus agreed 100% because it is a mechanical date rule. ActivityPattern agreed 85% (κ = 0.78), WhyRecovered 75%, RecentThemes 70%, and HasRecovered only 60% (κ = 0.31). The low HasRecovered score was mostly a coding problem, not a real difference in reading the data. In my own MP2 work I used qualified labels like "Partially", "Barely" and "Yes, briefly" even though the coding guide defines the field as strictly yes/no. Once we mapped those to the binary value, every HasRecovered disagreement on my projects went away.

Looking across the adjudicated labels, long gaps were rarely preceded by any warning sign in the commits. Before the gap, most projects were doing routine work: release prep in convex.jl and genomepy, fixes for an R internals change in permute, dependency pins in brial. Projects did not seem to wind down visibly before going quiet; maintainers simply ran out of pressing work. Recovery, on the other hand, usually had a clear external trigger. These included a new Python or Julia release forcing compatibility work (cyvcf2, rigidbodydynamics.jl), a broken dependency (genomepy's MySQL connection), a framework update breaking the UI (systempipeshiny's Shiny issue), and a maintainer deciding to modernize the codebase (chainconsumer's pydantic rewrite, brial's new autotools build). Who came back also varied. Sometimes the original maintainer returned alone (clafer's lead researcher after 50 months), and sometimes a new contributor took over (convex.jl, angel).

A gap also did not mean the project was dead. Every project with a qualifying gap had commits afterward, and 18 of the 20 projects are still Active under the April 2025 cutoff. Several projects with long gaps, like brial (27 months) and clafer (50 months), are still being maintained today, just at a low level. This is also why ActivityPattern needed care. A project can show steady decline and still be alive, so "declining" (for bmc, brial and rigidbodydynamics) fit better than "irregular" or "cyclical", which the coding guide reserves for unpredictable swings or repeating cycles at fixed intervals.

Three interpretation pitfalls came up repeatedly. First, short pauses got treated as real gaps. jasp-desktop's 1-month "gap" was labeled as a recovery even though the threshold is 3 months, so HasRecovered and WhyRecovered should both be N/A. Second, RecentThemes rankings were distorted by unlabeled merge commits. Coding "Merge X into Y" as "Other" let empty commits outrank the actual feature and documentation work those merges carried. We resolved this by classifying merges by the PR they brought in, or as automated bot contributions when they were machine-generated. Third, the validation process itself had gaps. jmurph91's RecentThemes check was based on my reported values rather than my projects' actual latest 10 commits, so his 100% agreement on that field is less meaningful than it looks. A data-loading bug also turned my "N/A" values into "Not reported". Both points suggest the codebook should require validators to re-derive each value from the raw commits, and should state explicitly how to code merge commits and projects with no qualifying gap.
